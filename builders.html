BTQ Technologies Infrastructure

First to demonstrate a quantum-safe Bitcoin implementation using NIST ML-DSA signatures. Bitcoin Quantum Core Release 0.2 protects the entire $2.4T Bitcoin market with production-ready code.

→ btq.com

Chaincode Labs Research

Published "Bitcoin and Quantum Computing: Current Status and Future Directions" paper. Leading cryptographers Tim Rufing, Jonas Nick, and Ethan Heilman are actively working on Bitcoin's quantum readiness.

→ Research Paper

Bitcoin Core Developers Protocol

BIP-360 authors Hunter Beast, Ethan Heilman, and Isabel Foxen Duke are proposing Pay-to-Merkle-Root (P2MR) — the first concrete Bitcoin quantum defense merged into the official BIP repository.

→ BIP-360 Specification

Project 11 (P11) Research

Quantum computing research org behind the Q-Day Prize — offering 1 BTC to the first team to break an elliptic curve cryptographic (ECC) key using a quantum computer. Launched April 2025 to benchmark the real-world quantum threat to Bitcoin.

→ projecteleven.com

Bitcoin Post-Quantum (BPQ) Fork

Bitcoin fork launched in 2018 with XMSS quantum-resistant signatures and Equihash PoW. One of the first implementations of post-quantum cryptography in a Bitcoin-based blockchain.

→ bitcoinpq.org

Blockstream Research Research

Led by Adam Back, working on hash-based signatures and post-quantum cryptographic solutions for Bitcoin. Contributing to quantum resistance research and BIP-360 development efforts.

→ blockstream.com

Standards & Government

Standards & Government

NIST: Finalized post-quantum cryptography standards (ML-KEM, ML-DSA, SLH-DSA) in August 2024 that Bitcoin projects are implementing.

QuINSA: Quantum Internet Standards Alliance where BTQ Technologies co-chairs quantum communications working group.

CNSA 2.0: NSA mandating post-quantum cryptography migration by 2030-2035, creating urgency across all crypto systems.

Development Approaches

The Bitcoin quantum resistance ecosystem is taking multiple parallel approaches:

Protocol Layer (BIP-360): New address types that hide public keys from quantum attacks. Conservative approach that doesn't require new cryptography yet — just better key hiding.
Wallet Layer (PQ-PSBT): Dual-signing with both classical and post-quantum keys. Works today without consensus changes. Provides "quantum insurance" for early adopters.
Fork/Testnet Approach: Bitcoin Cash is testing post-quantum vaults on Chipnet. Provides real-world testing ground for approaches that could be adapted to Bitcoin.
Infrastructure & Standards: BTQ and others are building the tooling, standards, and production implementations that Bitcoin could adopt when ready.

The Challenge Ahead

Building quantum resistance for Bitcoin isn't just a technical problem — it's a coordination problem. With over $2 trillion in market value and millions of users, any change must be:

  • Backward compatible: Can't break existing wallets or infrastructure
  • Gradual: Users can migrate at their own pace
  • Battle-tested: Extensively tested before activation
  • Conservative: Low risk of unintended consequences
  • Consensus-driven: Community must agree on the approach

The teams listed above are tackling these challenges from multiple angles — some focusing on immediate solutions, others on long-term protocol changes. The diversity of approaches increases Bitcoin's chances of being ready when quantum computers mature.
