The Quantum Threat to Bitcoin

Understanding how quantum computers could break Bitcoin's cryptography — and how far away we really are.

What Exactly Is the Threat?

Bitcoin uses Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECDSA/Schnorr) to secure transactions. Your private key signs transactions; your public key verifies them. The security relies on the fact that deriving a private key from a public key is computationally infeasible for classical computers.

Shor's Algorithm (1994) — running on a sufficiently powerful quantum computer — could solve this problem exponentially faster, potentially recovering private keys from public keys.

Two Types of Attack

Long Exposure Attack — The easier one. Attacker has unlimited time to crack public keys already visible on the blockchain. Targets: P2PK addresses (Satoshi's coins), reused addresses, and Taproot (P2TR) outputs where the public key is always exposed.
Short Exposure Attack — The harder one. Attacker must crack your key in the ~10 minutes between when you broadcast a transaction (revealing your public key) and when it's confirmed. Requires much faster quantum computers.

What's NOT Threatened

SHA-256 (mining/hashing) — Grover's algorithm gives only a quadratic speedup against hash functions, effectively halving the security bits (256→128). 128-bit security is still considered safe. Bitcoin's proof-of-work is NOT the weak point.

The gap between current capability and what's needed to threaten Bitcoin is enormous. Most estimates suggest a cryptographically relevant quantum computer (CRQC) is 10–30+ years away, though some government agencies plan for worst-case scenarios as early as 2030–2035.

Key reality check: Current quantum computers struggle with tasks that classical computers solve trivially. The jump from ~1,000 noisy physical qubits to millions of error-corrected logical qubits is not just a scaling problem — it requires fundamental breakthroughs in error correction, coherence, and architecture.
Major Development: Google's Quantum Echoes (Oct 2025) achieved verifiable quantum advantage — Willow chip ran OTOC algorithm 13,000× faster than fastest classical supercomputer. While not crypto-relevant, it's the first real-world quantum advantage on hardware.

Competing Quantum Computing Approaches

Different companies are pursuing fundamentally different quantum hardware architectures, each with unique paths to scale:

Superconducting Qubits
Google, IBM, Rigetti

Most mature technology. Google Willow (105 qubits) achieved below-threshold error correction. IBM Condor reached 1,121 qubits. Requires dilution refrigeration (~mK). Path to scale: modular architecture, improved coherence.

Topological Qubits
Microsoft

Microsoft Majorana 1: 8 topological qubits using topoconductors. Inherently error-resistant at hardware level. Claims path to 1M qubits on palm-sized chip. Could dramatically compress timelines if it works at scale.

Trapped Ion
Quantinuum, IonQ

Highest gate fidelities, any-to-any connectivity. Quantinuum H2 has 56 qubits. Slower gates but more precise. Path to scale: larger traps, improved lasers, parallel processing.

Photonic
Xanadu, PsiQuantum

Room temperature operation, network-native. PsiQuantum building 1M+ photonic qubit system. Challenges: probabilistic gates, photon loss. Path to scale: improved sources, detectors.

Why this matters: If any approach achieves a breakthrough (especially topological), it could accelerate quantum computing timelines significantly. Bitcoin's quantum preparedness must account for multiple potential paths to CRQC.

The Bottom Line

The quantum threat to Bitcoin is real but not imminent. Current quantum computers are nowhere near the scale needed to break elliptic curve cryptography. However, the exponential nature of potential breakthroughs means the timeline could compress rapidly.

The key question isn't whether quantum computers will eventually threaten Bitcoin — it's whether Bitcoin will be ready when they do. Microsoft's topological breakthrough and other advances show that multiple paths to quantum advantage exist, making preparedness essential.
