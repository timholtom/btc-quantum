Caltech/Oratomic: Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computer May Need Only 10K–20K Qubits

Caltech and Oratomic publish a new quantum error correction architecture for neutral atom platforms that reduces the physical-to-logical qubit ratio from ~1,000:1 to ~5:1 — a 200× improvement. A fully fault-tolerant quantum computer could be built with as few as 10,000–20,000 qubits, not the millions previously estimated. Built on Caltech's existing 6,100-qubit array. John Preskill: "Now at last we're getting close." The team expects fault-tolerant machines by end of decade.

NEW

Google: Breaking Bitcoin Requires Fewer Qubits Than Thought, Taproot Partly to Blame

Google's Quantum AI team publishes whitepaper (with Ethereum's Justin Drake as late co-author) showing Bitcoin's cryptography could be broken with fewer than 500,000 physical qubits using ~1,200 logical qubits and ~100M Toffoli gates — far below the "millions" often cited. Taproot's design exposes public keys by default, expanding the quantum attack surface. A mid-transaction attack model could derive private keys in ~9 minutes (before Bitcoin's ~10 min confirmation), giving attackers a 41% success rate. ~6.9M BTC (~⅓ of supply) already sits in addresses with exposed public keys. Google used zero-knowledge proofs for responsible disclosure — withholding actual circuits.

NEW

ArXiv Paper: Quantum Mining Requires Kardashev Type II Energy — Physically Impossible

New academic paper (arXiv:2603.25519) provides the first end-to-end cost analysis of quantum Bitcoin mining via Grover's algorithm. At Bitcoin's current difficulty, a quantum mining fleet would require ~10²³ qubits and ~10²⁵ watts — approaching Kardashev Type II (entire star's output). Even at the most favorable settings, ~10⁸ qubits and ~10⁴ MW are needed (a national grid). Conclusively settles: SHA-256 mining is not a viable quantum attack vector. The real threat remains Shor's algorithm against ECDSA signatures.
