BREAKING

CoinDesk: Bitcoin's $1.3 Trillion Quantum-Proofing Race — Full Initiative Breakdown

CoinDesk publishes a comprehensive technical breakdown of every major quantum-proofing initiative for Bitcoin. Covers BIP-360 (P2MR removing on-chain public keys), SPHINCS+/SLH-DSA (hash-based PQ signatures at 8KB+, standardized as FIPS 205), new proposals SHRIMPS and SHRINCS that aim to shrink PQ signature sizes while retaining SPHINCS+ security, Tadge Dryja's commit/reveal soft fork (two-phase transactions where a pre-registered hash "alibi" blocks quantum forgeries in the mempool), and Hunter Beast's Hourglass V2 (gradually restricting the ~1.7M BTC in exposed legacy addresses). Notes Google research shows a quantum computer could crack Bitcoin's cryptography in under 9 minutes — faster than the average block time. Some analysts place the threat as early as 2029.

BREAKING

Quantum-Safe Bitcoin Without a Fork: StarkWare QSB + Lightning Labs Escape Hatch

StarkWare CPO Avihu Levy publishes QSB (Quantum Safe Bitcoin) — the first scheme to make Bitcoin transactions quantum-resistant without any protocol change, using hash-to-sig puzzles within existing legacy script rules. Costs $75–$150/tx in GPU compute, making it a "last resort" for large transfers. Lightning Labs CTO Olaoluwa Osuntokun separately publishes a quantum "escape hatch" prototype enabling wallet ownership proof from seed phrases without revealing keys. Meanwhile, Bernstein gives Bitcoin a 3–5 year window, calling quantum risk a "manageable upgrade cycle." Together, these show meaningful quantum defense is possible today — even before BIP-360.

Nobel Prize–winning physicist John Martinis — who led Google's quantum hardware program and the 2019 "quantum supremacy" experiment — warns CoinDesk that Bitcoin may be among the earliest real-world targets of quantum computing. Bitcoin Devs Warn Against Rushing Quantum Fixes — Mow, Back & Schnelli Push Back

A growing counter-movement among Bitcoin developers warns that rushing post-quantum fixes could be more dangerous than the quantum threat itself. Developer Jonas Schnelli warns PQ signatures are far less battle-tested than current cryptography: "The cure, rushed, could be worse than the disease." BIP-360 referenced as a measured, optional approach.

Google: Breaking Bitcoin Requires Fewer Qubits Than Thought, Taproot Partly to Blame

Google's Quantum AI team publishes whitepaper (with Ethereum's Justin Drake as late co-author) showing Bitcoin's cryptography could be broken with fewer than 500,000 physical qubits using ~1,200 logical qubits and ~100M Toffoli gates — far below the "millions" often cited. Google used zero-knowledge proofs for responsible disclosure — withholding actual circuits.

NEW

ArXiv Paper: Quantum Mining Requires Kardashev Type II Energy — Physically Impossible

New academic paper (arXiv:2603.25519) provides the first end-to-end cost analysis of quantum Bitcoin mining via Grover's algorithm. At Bitcoin's current difficulty, a quantum mining fleet would require ~10²³ qubits and ~10²⁵ watts — approaching Kardashev Type II (entire star's output). Even at the most favorable settings, ~10⁸ qubits and ~10⁴ MW are needed (a national grid). Conclusively settles: SHA-256 mining is not a viable quantum attack vector. The real threat remains Shor's algorithm against ECDSA signatures.
