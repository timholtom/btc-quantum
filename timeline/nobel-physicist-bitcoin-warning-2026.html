Nobel Physicist Warns Bitcoin Is Early Quantum Target

April 2026 — 2025 Nobel laureate John Martinis says Bitcoin could be cracked in minutes, gives the community 5–10 years to prepare.

← Back to Timeline

What Happened

John Martinis — the physicist who led the team behind Google's Sycamore quantum supremacy demonstration in 2019 and co-won the 2025 Nobel Prize in Physics for foundational contributions to quantum computing — publicly warned that Bitcoin could be one of the first high-value targets for a cryptographically relevant quantum computer (CRQC).

In interviews reported by CoinDesk, Bloomberg, and other outlets in early April 2026, Martinis stated that once a sufficiently powerful quantum computer exists, Bitcoin's elliptic curve signatures could be broken "in minutes." He emphasised that the community has a window of approximately 5–10 years to transition to quantum-resistant cryptography, but cautioned that the window may be narrower than many assume.

The warning carries exceptional weight. Martinis is not a commentator speculating from the sidelines — he is one of the people who built the hardware. His team created the Sycamore chip (53 qubits, 2019) and was instrumental in the Willow chip program (105 qubits, 2024). He was also appointed to the White House Science and Technology Panel in March 2026, giving his statements policy significance.

Why This Matters

This is the first time a Nobel Prize-winning physicist — and one of the architects of practical quantum computing — has singled out Bitcoin as a specific near-term target. Previous warnings from academics and industry figures often hedged with "decades away" timelines. Martinis's framing is notably more urgent:

  • Credibility: Nobel laureate, Google Quantum AI co-founder, White House advisor — not an outsider making predictions
  • Specificity: Named Bitcoin explicitly, not just "cryptography" or "encryption" in general
  • Timeline: 5–10 years is tighter than the "15–30 years" academic consensus cited as recently as 2024
  • Mechanism: Confirmed that exposed ECDSA public keys (P2PK, reused P2PKH, Taproot P2TR) are the primary attack surface

The warning arrived amid a cascade of quantum-Bitcoin news in late March and early April 2026 — including Google's Taproot quantum whitepaper, Caltech/Oratomic's 10K-qubit error correction breakthrough, and Cloudflare accelerating its PQC deadline to 2029. Martinis's Nobel prestige amplified the entire discussion, pushing quantum risk into mainstream financial coverage at Bloomberg, Fortune, and the Financial Times.

Context: The Accelerating Timeline

Martinis's warning came just weeks after several breakthroughs compressed the estimated timeline for CRQCs:

  • Google Quantum AI (Mar 31): Breaking Bitcoin's ECC may need fewer than 500,000 physical qubits
  • Caltech/Oratomic (Mar 30): New error correction reduces physical-to-logical ratio from ~1,000:1 to ~5:1, suggesting ~10,000 qubits could suffice for fault-tolerant computation
  • Global Risk Institute: Expert survey now puts the probability of a CRQC within 10 years at 49% — up from 31% in 2023
  • Science News (Apr 1): Headline "Just 10,000 quantum bits might crack internet encryption schemes" — reflecting the post-Oratomic consensus shift

Martinis essentially validated the most aggressive end of these estimates, telling reporters that advances in error correction have moved the timeline forward faster than most people in the field expected.

Reaction

The Bitcoin community's response was mixed. Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong, already leading a quantum-resistance coalition, cited Martinis's warning as validation of the urgency. Adam Back (Blockstream CEO) acknowledged the risk but counselled against rushing protocol changes. Samson Mow pushed back against "quantum panic," warning that hasty upgrades could introduce new vulnerabilities.

In traditional finance, Bernstein analysts published a note saying the quantum threat to Bitcoin is "real but manageable," estimating 3–5 years for the community to deploy post-quantum signatures. BlackRock's earlier quantum risk disclosure in its IBIT ETF filing took on new significance.
