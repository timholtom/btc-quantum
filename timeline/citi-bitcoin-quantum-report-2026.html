Citi Warns Bitcoin Faces Outsized Quantum Threat

May 2026 — Wall Street bank Citigroup warns that accelerating quantum computing breakthroughs are compressing the risk timeline, with Bitcoin seen as particularly exposed.

← Back to Timeline

What Happened

On May 18, 2026, Citigroup published an analyst report warning that advances in quantum computing are "compressing the timeline" for risks to cryptocurrency security and broader internet infrastructure. Analyst Alex Saunders singled out Bitcoin as particularly exposed compared to other blockchain networks.

"While large-scale quantum attacks remain a medium-term concern, the pace of progress has shortened the horizon and warrants closer attention from investors," Saunders wrote.

The report highlighted several key vulnerabilities specific to Bitcoin:

  • Conservative governance: Bitcoin's decentralized, consensus-driven upgrade process means protocol changes take years to implement — a structural disadvantage when facing an accelerating threat
  • Exposed public keys: An estimated 6.5–6.9 million BTC (~⅓ of circulating supply, roughly $450 billion) sit in addresses with already-exposed public keys, including wallets believed to belong to Satoshi Nakamoto
  • Mid-transaction attacks: Future quantum computers could potentially derive private keys fast enough to interfere with Bitcoin transactions before confirmation
  • "Harvest now, decrypt later": Attackers may already be collecting encrypted data and blockchain transactions for future quantum-enabled decryption

Bitcoin vs. Ethereum

The report drew a notable contrast between Bitcoin and proof-of-stake networks like Ethereum, arguing that Ethereum may be better positioned to respond because it can upgrade protocols more frequently. However, Saunders cautioned that sufficiently advanced quantum systems could compromise validator keys and disrupt any blockchain network's operations.

Why This Matters

Citi joins BlackRock (February 2026 ETF filing), Ark Invest (March 2026 report), and Galaxy Digital (March 2026 analysis) in a growing chorus of major financial institutions formally warning about Bitcoin's quantum exposure. The pattern is clear: Wall Street is no longer dismissing quantum risk as theoretical.

What makes Citi's report distinctive is its focus on governance risk — the argument that Bitcoin's greatest quantum vulnerability isn't technical but structural. Even if quantum-resistant cryptography exists (it does — NIST finalized standards in August 2024), Bitcoin's slow, contentious upgrade process may prevent timely deployment.

This echoes Project Eleven's landmark report from earlier in May 2026, which argued "the gap is not technical — it's coordination." Citi's institutional endorsement of this view adds significant weight from traditional finance.

Constructive Outlook

Despite the warnings, Citi's report remained constructive on crypto's long-term ability to adapt through post-quantum cryptography and protocol redesigns. The bank specifically cited BIP-360 (P2MR quantum-resistant addresses) and BIP-361 (legacy signature sunset) as developments worth monitoring.

The institutional pattern: BlackRock flags risk → Ark Invest quantifies it → Galaxy Digital maps the response → Citi warns governance is the bottleneck. Each successive report narrows the focus from "is this a problem?" to "can Bitcoin's governance move fast enough?"
