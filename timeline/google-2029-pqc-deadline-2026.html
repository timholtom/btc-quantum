Google Sets 2029 Post-Quantum Deadline

March 25, 2026 — Google publishes a formal timeline to migrate all infrastructure to post-quantum cryptography by 2029, calling the quantum threat imminent.

← Back to Timeline

What Happened

On March 25, 2026, Google published a formal blog post signed by VP of Security Engineering Heather Adkins and Senior Cryptography Engineer Sophie Schmieg, committing to transitioning Google's entire infrastructure to post-quantum cryptography (PQC) by 2029.

The announcement warned that quantum frontiers "may be closer than they appear" and described the 2029 target as a response to rapid advances in quantum hardware, error correction, and factoring resource estimates. Google stated: "Quantum computers will pose a significant threat to current cryptographic standards, and specifically to encryption and digital signature."

As a concrete first step, Google announced that Android 17 will integrate post-quantum digital signature protection using ML-DSA (FIPS 204), the signature algorithm recently standardized by NIST. The company is also deploying PQC across Google Cloud and all internal communications systems.

Why This Matters

Google's announcement is significant for several reasons:

"Harvest Now, Decrypt Later" Is Already Happening

Google explicitly acknowledged that adversaries are already stealing encrypted data today, planning to decrypt it once quantum computers are powerful enough. This threat is present-tense, not theoretical.

Compressed Resource Estimates

Google's own researchers recently found that cracking RSA encryption may require 20× fewer quantum resources than previously estimated. Separately, Iceberg Quantum now suggests that breaking RSA-2048 could require as few as ~100,000 qubits — down from earlier estimates of 20 million. While these are RSA estimates (not ECC/Bitcoin directly), they signal that security margins across all public-key cryptography are shrinking faster than expected.

Industry Convergence on 2029

The 2029 deadline is not arbitrary. IBM has its own roadmap targeting fault-tolerant quantum systems by the same year. Both of the world's largest quantum computing companies are converging on the same timeline for when quantum machines become cryptographically dangerous.

Impact on Bitcoin

Bitcoin runs on elliptic curve cryptography (ECDSA with secp256k1) — the same class of math that quantum computers running Shor's algorithm could eventually reverse. Given a public key, a sufficiently powerful quantum machine could derive the corresponding private key.

The asymmetry between Google and Bitcoin is stark:

  • Google controls its own infrastructure — it can mandate a migration timeline and enforce compliance across its services
  • Bitcoin is decentralized — miners, wallet developers, exchanges, and millions of individual users would all need to coordinate a migration
  • Bitcoin consensus changes take years — Jameson Lopp estimates migrating billions in user funds could take 5-10 years on its own

According to Project Eleven, over 6.8 million Bitcoin (~$470 billion) sits in addresses vulnerable to quantum attacks, including coins from Bitcoin's earliest days. Ark Invest estimates roughly 35% of total Bitcoin supply is in theoretically vulnerable address types.

The Core Problem: Google can set a 2029 deadline because it controls its own infrastructure. Bitcoin cannot. This is an externally-imposed deadline the network didn't set for itself and can't afford to ignore. If Google believes it needs to be post-quantum ready by 2029, the pressure on Bitcoin to have a credible migration path intensifies significantly.

Current Bitcoin Defenses

Bitcoin developers are not idle. Key defensive efforts include:

  • BIP-360 (P2MR) — quantum-resistant address format now live on testnet with 100K+ transactions
  • Legacy Signature Sunset BIP — proposal to phase out vulnerable signature types and eventually freeze quantum-exposed UTXOs
  • Active community discussion — ongoing debate on bitcoin-dev mailing list about urgency vs. caution
