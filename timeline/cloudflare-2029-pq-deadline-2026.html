Cloudflare Targets 2029 for Full Post-Quantum Security

April 7, 2026 — The company that secures ~20% of the internet accelerates its PQ migration by six years, citing "shockingly low" qubit requirements from Oratomic. IBM warns of quantum attacks as early as 2029.

← Back to Timeline

What Happened

On April 7, 2026, Cloudflare — which secures approximately 20% of all websites and a significant fraction of internet traffic — announced it was accelerating its post-quantum migration deadline from its previous NIST-aligned 2035 target to 2029. The announcement came directly in response to two simultaneous breakthroughs published the week prior: Google's quantum cryptography whitepaper and Oratomic's resource estimates for breaking encryption on neutral atom computers.

Cloudflare's Bas Westerbaan, a cybersecurity researcher at the company, told TIME: "It's a real shock. We'll need to speed up our efforts considerably." He added: "Almost every system in the world becomes vulnerable altogether to a quantum attacker."

The same day, IBM Quantum Safe's CTO Ray Harishankar took an even more pessimistic view, stating he could not rule out quantum "moonshot attacks" on high-value targets as early as 2029. This marked the first time a major quantum computing company acknowledged potential near-term cryptographic attacks.

Why Cloudflare Accelerated

Three independent lines of progress converged to force Cloudflare's hand:

1. Oratomic's "shockingly low" qubit estimate: Oratomic showed that breaking P-256 (the elliptic curve standard used across the internet, closely related to Bitcoin's secp256k1) requires only ~10,000 neutral atom qubits — thanks to a new error correction approach needing just 3–4 physical qubits per logical qubit, down from ~1,000:1.
2. Google's undisclosed algorithm improvement: Google revealed (via zero-knowledge proof) a major speedup to the algorithm for breaking elliptic curve cryptography, without publishing the actual algorithm — a first in responsible quantum disclosure.
3. Researchers going dark: Cloudflare cited quantum computing scientist Scott Aaronson's late-2025 warning: "At some point, the people doing detailed estimates of how many physical qubits it'll take to break actually deployed cryptosystems are going to stop publishing those estimates… for all we know, that point may have been passed already." Cloudflare concluded: "That point has now passed indeed."

Key Technical Insights

Cloudflare's analysis highlighted that progress on three independent fronts — hardware, error correction, and quantum software — compounds multiplicatively. A breakthrough in any one area accelerates the overall timeline. In March–April 2026, breakthroughs occurred in all three simultaneously:

  • Hardware: Neutral atom platforms (Caltech's 6,100-qubit array) proving more scalable than expected
  • Error correction: Oratomic's 3–4 physical qubits per logical qubit (vs. ~1,000 for superconducting)
  • Software: Google's improved algorithm for breaking ECC, plus Oratomic's architecture-specific optimizations

Crucially, Cloudflare noted a shift in Google's priorities: Google now prioritizes quantum-secure authentication over harvest-now/decrypt-later protection. This indicates Google believes Q-Day could arrive before 2030 — authentication only matters if the quantum threat is imminent, not distant.

The "Researchers Going Dark" Warning

Perhaps the most unsettling aspect of Cloudflare's announcement was its acknowledgment that the era of open quantum research may be ending. With practical cryptographic attacks becoming feasible, the incentive to publish detailed attack methodologies is disappearing. This means:

  • Future quantum breakthroughs may not be publicly announced
  • Nation-states and well-funded labs may develop cryptographic attacks in secret
  • The public timeline for Q-Day may lag the actual timeline by years
  • Google's use of zero-knowledge proofs to disclose without revealing is the new model

Impact on Bitcoin

Cloudflare's 2029 deadline and IBM's warning carry significant implications for Bitcoin:

Timeline compression: When Cloudflare (which already has 65%+ of traffic PQ-encrypted) says it needs to rush, it signals the threat is closer than most Bitcoin community members assume. Bitcoin has no equivalent migration plan or deadline.
Authentication urgency: Google's focus on PQ authentication directly parallels Bitcoin's vulnerability — ECDSA signatures are an authentication mechanism. If Google believes authentication must be secured by 2029, Bitcoin's ECDSA is on the same clock.
Information asymmetry: As researchers stop publishing quantum attack improvements, Bitcoin's community will have less visibility into the actual threat timeline. The "we'll have years of warning" assumption may be wrong.

Over 65% of Cloudflare's human traffic is already post-quantum encrypted for key exchange. But authentication — the equivalent of Bitcoin's signature scheme — remains the harder, unsolved problem. Cloudflare's urgency around authentication mirrors the exact challenge Bitcoin faces with ECDSA.

Industry Context

Cloudflare's move followed Google's own 2029 PQ deadline (announced March 25, 2026) and came alongside a cascade of institutional responses to the Google/Oratomic papers. The pattern of 2029 as an industry convergence point — Google, Cloudflare, and IBM all pointing to the same year — represents the most concrete timeline shift in the quantum threat landscape to date.
