Google: Breaking Bitcoin Requires Fewer Qubits Than Thought

March 31, 2026 — Google's Quantum AI team publishes whitepaper showing Bitcoin's cryptography could fall to fewer than 500,000 qubits. Taproot's design makes matters worse.

← Back to Timeline

What Happened

On March 31, 2026, Google's Quantum AI team published a blog post and whitepaper revealing that cracking the cryptography protecting Bitcoin and Ethereum could require fewer than 500,000 physical qubits — well below the "millions" commonly cited by the crypto industry.

The researchers designed two potential attack methods, each requiring roughly 1,200 to 1,450 high-quality logical qubits. When accounting for the physical-to-logical qubit overhead from error correction, this translates to fewer than 500,000 physical qubits — a fraction of earlier estimates that placed the threshold at 13 million or more.

The Mid-Transaction Attack

Perhaps the most striking finding is a practical attack model targeting transactions in real time:

How It Works

When someone sends bitcoin, a public key is briefly revealed during the transaction broadcast. A sufficiently powerful quantum computer could:

  1. Pre-compute part of the Shor's algorithm attack in advance
  2. Complete the private key derivation in approximately 9 minutes once the transaction appears
  3. Submit a competing transaction stealing the funds before the original confirms

Since Bitcoin transactions take ~10 minutes to confirm, this gives an attacker roughly a 41% success rate per attempt.

This "short exposure" attack model is particularly concerning because it can target any Bitcoin transaction, not just old wallets with exposed public keys. Even users following best practices (new address per transaction, no address reuse) would be vulnerable.

The Taproot Problem

The whitepaper also casts new light on Bitcoin's 2021 Taproot upgrade (BIP-341):

Taproot Exposes Public Keys by Default

Unlike older address formats (P2PKH, P2SH) which hash the public key — providing an extra layer of protection — Taproot's P2TR addresses expose a tweaked public key directly on the blockchain. This was a deliberate design choice for efficiency and privacy, but it removes a layer of quantum defense.

Google's researchers say this design choice could expand the number of wallets vulnerable to future quantum attacks, turning Taproot into a quantum liability rather than a privacy improvement.

Scale of Vulnerability

The paper estimates approximately 6.9 million bitcoin (~one-third of total supply) already sit in wallets where the public key has been exposed in some way:

  • ~1.7 million BTC from the network's early years (P2PK outputs)
  • Funds in addresses that have been reused (public key revealed from prior spends)
  • All Taproot (P2TR) addresses, which expose public keys by design

This figure is significantly higher than CoinShares' recent estimate of only ~10,200 BTC being concentrated enough to significantly move markets if stolen.

Responsible Disclosure via Zero-Knowledge Proofs

Notably, Google used a novel approach to sharing this sensitive research:

Zero-Knowledge Proof Disclosure

Rather than publishing step-by-step attack instructions, Google's team used zero-knowledge proofs to demonstrate their findings are accurate without revealing the specific methodology. This allows independent verification of the results while limiting misuse potential — a first for quantum cryptanalysis research disclosure.

Why This Matters for Bitcoin

The Gap Is Shrinking Faster Than Expected

If breaking Bitcoin requires ~500K physical qubits instead of 13M+, the timeline to a cryptographically relevant quantum computer compresses significantly. Combined with Google's own 2029 PQC migration deadline and rapid hardware progress, this reinforces the urgency of Bitcoin's quantum defense preparations.

  • Previous estimate: 13M+ physical qubits → comfortably decades away
  • New estimate: <500K physical qubits → potentially within the 2030s hardware roadmaps
  • Ethereum may be less exposed to the mid-transaction attack due to faster block times (~12 seconds vs ~10 minutes)
  • Taproot adoption is expanding the quantum attack surface, not shrinking it
