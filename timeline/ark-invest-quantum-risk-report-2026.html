Ark Invest Publishes Bitcoin Quantum Risk Report

March 2026 — Cathie Wood's Ark Invest publishes a comprehensive quantum risk assessment, concluding the threat is real but distant, with ~35% of Bitcoin supply in theoretically vulnerable address types.

← Back to Timeline

What Happened

On March 12, 2026, Cathie Wood's Ark Invest published a detailed research report examining quantum computing's risk to Bitcoin. The report concluded that while quantum computing represents a legitimate long-term risk, it is not an imminent threat to Bitcoin's security. Current quantum computers lack the processing power needed to break Bitcoin's elliptic curve cryptography, and any meaningful breakthrough would likely compromise broader internet security infrastructure first — providing early warning signals.

The report quantified Bitcoin's exposure for the first time from a major investment research firm: approximately 35% of the total Bitcoin supply sits in address types that are theoretically vulnerable to future quantum attacks. These include early Pay-to-Public-Key (P2PK) addresses where public keys are directly exposed on-chain, as well as addresses that have been reused after spending (exposing their public keys). Of this vulnerable portion, roughly 1.7 million BTC is likely already permanently lost — coins from Bitcoin's earliest days that will never be moved regardless of quantum developments.

Critically, the report argued that quantum risk would not arrive as a sudden surprise. Any quantum threat would unfold through visible technological milestones over years, giving Bitcoin developers ample time to implement post-quantum cryptographic upgrades. The report specifically referenced proposals like BIP-360 as evidence that the Bitcoin development community is already preparing defensive measures.

Key Findings

  • ~35% of Bitcoin supply is in address types theoretically vulnerable to quantum attacks
  • ~1.7 million BTC of that amount is likely already permanently lost
  • Current quantum computers are far from capable of breaking Bitcoin's ECDSA
  • Broader internet security (banking, TLS, etc.) would be affected first
  • Quantum breakthroughs would emerge through visible milestones, not overnight
  • Bitcoin has upgrade pathways (BIP-360, post-quantum signatures) ready to deploy

Why This Matters

Ark Invest is one of the most prominent and closely-watched investment firms in the crypto space, managing billions in Bitcoin-related assets. Cathie Wood has been one of Bitcoin's most vocal institutional advocates. For Ark to publish a dedicated quantum risk assessment signals that the topic has moved from niche technical debate to mainstream institutional concern.

The report's measured tone — acknowledging the risk while pushing back against panic — provides a counterweight to more alarmist narratives. Coming just weeks after BlackRock flagged quantum risk in its ETF filing, the Ark report suggests a growing consensus among major institutional players: quantum is a real consideration that needs monitoring and preparation, but not an imminent crisis.

The 35% vulnerability figure, while not new to researchers, carries additional weight coming from a major investment firm. It provides a concrete data point for institutional risk models and could influence how funds and asset managers price quantum risk into their Bitcoin exposure calculations.

Context

The Ark report arrives during a period of heightened institutional attention to Bitcoin's quantum risk. In January 2026, Jefferies strategist Christopher Wood swapped his 10% Bitcoin allocation for gold specifically over quantum concerns. In February, BlackRock added quantum risk language to its IBIT ETF filing. Meanwhile, Wall Street broker Benchmark argued the threat is "real but distant," and Galaxy CEO Mike Novogratz downplayed the risk.

The emerging pattern is clear: quantum risk is now a standard part of institutional Bitcoin analysis, even as experts disagree on timelines. The Ark report contributes to a more nuanced, data-driven conversation about how to think about — and prepare for — the quantum threat.
