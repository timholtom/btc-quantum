US Commerce Dept: $2B CHIPS Act Quantum Investment

May 21, 2026 — The largest single US government investment in quantum computing, funding 9 companies across every major qubit modality.

← Back to Timeline

What Happened

The US Department of Commerce announced $2.013 billion in federal incentives under the CHIPS and Science Act for 9 quantum computing companies. The funding is split between two quantum foundry companies and seven quantum computing companies, targeting the "most critical technology challenges in the race to develop utility-scale, fault-tolerant quantum computers."

This represents the largest single tranche of US government funding dedicated to quantum computing, signaling that the Trump Administration views quantum supremacy as a national security priority on par with semiconductor manufacturing.

Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick stated: "With today's CHIPS Research and Development investments in quantum computing, the Trump administration is leading the world into a new era of American innovation."

Who Gets What

Quantum Foundries — $1.375B
  • IBM — $1 billion: New quantum foundry subsidiary for quantum-grade superconducting wafers, building on US leadership in superconducting quantum wafer fabrication
  • GlobalFoundries — $375 million: Secure domestic quantum foundry supporting multiple modalities (superconducting, trapped ion, photonic, topological, silicon spin)
Quantum Computing Companies — $638M
  • Atom Computing — $100M: Neutral-atom hardware for tens of thousands of qubits
  • D-Wave — $100M: Annealing and gate-model superconducting systems, error rates, coherence
  • Infleqtion — $100M: Large-scale neutral-atom architectures, optical systems, error correction
  • PsiQuantum — $100M: Photonic quantum computing, single-photon detectors, low-loss packaging
  • Quantinuum — $100M: Fault-tolerant trapped-ion computers, integrated photonics
  • Rigetti — $100M: Next-gen superconducting tech, miniaturized readout electronics, cryostat architectures
  • Diraq — $38M: Silicon spin quantum computing, large-scale qubit arrays

The Department will receive a minority, non-controlling equity stake in each company as a condition for receiving funds — meaning US taxpayers now have a direct financial interest in accelerating quantum computing capabilities.

Why Every Major Modality Matters

The portfolio approach is deliberate: rather than betting on one quantum architecture, the government is funding all five leading modalities simultaneously — neutral atom, silicon-spin, superconducting, photonic, and trapped ion. This dramatically increases the probability that at least one pathway reaches fault-tolerant, utility-scale quantum computing within the decade.

Each modality has different strengths. Superconducting qubits (IBM, Google) are the most mature. Neutral atoms (Atom Computing, Infleqtion) recently demonstrated 6,100+ qubit arrays. Trapped ions (Quantinuum) offer the highest gate fidelities. Photonic systems (PsiQuantum) promise room-temperature operation. Topological qubits (Microsoft Majorana, via GlobalFoundries) could offer inherent error protection.

Impact on Bitcoin's Timeline

This investment directly accelerates every pathway to a cryptographically relevant quantum computer (CRQC). Key implications:

Timeline Compression: $2B in dedicated funding — with government equity stakes creating aligned incentives — could compress quantum development timelines by years. Companies receiving CHIPS funding are now racing with both private investment and federal backing.
  • IBM's $1B foundry directly enables manufacturing the quantum-grade wafers needed for the ~500,000 physical qubits Google estimates could break Bitcoin
  • PsiQuantum's $100M adds to the $750M+ already committed to its Chicago 1M-qubit facility
  • Atom Computing and Infleqtion are building on the Caltech 6,100-qubit neutral atom breakthrough — the same architecture that Caltech/Oratomic showed could achieve fault tolerance with just 10K-20K qubits
  • All modalities funded simultaneously means the "which architecture wins?" uncertainty works against Bitcoin — any one breaking through is enough

Combined with Google's March 2026 whitepaper showing Bitcoin needs only ~500K physical qubits to break (down from millions), and Caltech/Oratomic's April 2026 paper showing fault tolerance may need only 10K-20K qubits, this funding round turns theoretical roadmaps into funded engineering programs.

Context: Funding doesn't equal capability. The DARPA US2QC program, Caltech breakthroughs, and now CHIPS Act funding all point in the same direction — but building a CRQC still requires solving fundamental engineering challenges in error correction, coherence, and connectivity that money alone can't buy. The consensus timeline (2030-2040) may compress, but likely not to "next year."
