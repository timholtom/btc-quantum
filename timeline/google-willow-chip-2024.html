Google Willow Chip

December 2024 — 105 qubits with breakthrough error correction that crossed the quantum threshold.

← Back to Timeline

What Happened

Google announced its Willow quantum chip, featuring 105 superconducting qubits and a breakthrough in quantum error correction. For the first time, Willow demonstrated "below threshold" error correction — as more qubits were added to the system, the overall error rate decreased rather than increased. This milestone had been a holy grail of quantum computing for decades.

The achievement proved that quantum error correction could work in practice, not just theory. Willow's qubits maintained coherence for nearly 100 microseconds, five times longer than Google's previous generation. The chip successfully demonstrated that quantum computers could suppress errors faster than they accumulate, a requirement for building larger, more powerful quantum systems.

While still far from cryptographically relevant capabilities, Willow represented a crucial step toward scalable quantum computing. The error correction breakthrough provided a clear path for building larger quantum computers with millions of qubits, though significant engineering challenges remain.

Technical Significance

Willow's breakthrough lay in crossing the quantum error correction threshold, where adding more qubits to error correction codes reduces rather than increases the logical error rate. Previous quantum systems suffered from error rates that increased with system size, making large-scale quantum computing impossible.

The chip used surface codes for quantum error correction, arranging physical qubits in a 2D lattice where neighboring qubits monitor each other for errors. Willow demonstrated that these codes could successfully suppress errors in real hardware, not just computer simulations.

Google's team showed exponential suppression of logical error rates as they increased the code size from 3x3 to 5x5 to 7x7 qubit arrays. This scaling behavior suggests that even larger quantum error correction codes would continue to improve logical qubit fidelity, enabling fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Impact on Bitcoin's Timeline

Willow's error correction success validated the fundamental approach needed for cryptographically relevant quantum computers. While 105 qubits are orders of magnitude away from the millions needed to break Bitcoin's cryptography, the breakthrough proved that scaling to such sizes was theoretically and practically feasible.

The achievement also enabled Google's subsequent Quantum Echoes research, demonstrating practical quantum advantage in molecular simulation. This success attracted increased investment and attention to quantum computing, accelerating development timelines across the industry.

However, Willow's capabilities remain far from threatening Bitcoin. Breaking secp256k1 elliptic curve cryptography would require approximately 4 million logical qubits, each composed of roughly 1,000 physical qubits like those in Willow. This suggests that cryptographically relevant quantum computers remain at least 10-15 years away, even with continued breakthrough progress.
