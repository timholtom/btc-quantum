Coinbase CEO Leads Industry Quantum-Resistance Coalition

April 2026 — Brian Armstrong takes personal oversight of Bitcoin's post-quantum transition, forming a coalition with Block, TBD, and Bitcoin Core contributors.

← Back to Timeline

What Happened

Coinbase CEO and co-founder Brian Armstrong announced at the Security at Scale summit that he would personally lead an industry coalition to accelerate Bitcoin's transition to quantum-resistant cryptography. The move marked a significant shift — from treating quantum computing as a distant risk to treating it as a defined, near-term engineering priority.

Armstrong's announcement came in the wake of Google Quantum AI's March 2026 whitepaper showing a hypothetical 500,000-qubit system could crack Bitcoin's elliptic curve cryptography in under nine minutes. While such hardware doesn't yet exist, the shrinking resource estimates galvanized action from the crypto industry's biggest players.

The coalition includes engineers from Block (formerly Square), Jack Dorsey's TBD, and contributors to the Bitcoin Core project. The group is framing the effort as a foundational upgrade to Bitcoin's security model, with implications extending beyond retail users to institutional holders and custodians.

The Technical Approach

At the core of the coalition's proposal is BIP-360, which introduces Winternitz One-Time Signatures — a form of hash-based cryptography widely viewed as resistant to quantum attacks. Unlike the current Elliptic Curve Digital Signature Algorithm (ECDSA), which is vulnerable to Shor's Algorithm on a sufficiently powerful quantum computer, Winternitz signatures rely on hash functions that are not known to be susceptible to quantum speedups of the same magnitude.

Hash-based signatures are larger and require more computation, raising concerns about transaction size, network throughput, and usability. To address this, the coalition's developers are testing a dual-signature approach that would allow users to migrate funds from legacy addresses into new quantum-safe formats over time, rather than forcing an abrupt transition.

This gradual migration model aligns with StarkWare's QSB scheme and Lightning Labs' "escape hatch" prototype — solutions that work today without protocol changes but at higher cost. BIP-360 would provide the long-term, protocol-level solution.

Quantum-Proof Custody by Late 2026

Coinbase announced plans to introduce "quantum-proof" custody services for institutional clients by late 2026. These would combine existing encryption methods with emerging post-quantum standards under review by NIST and other standards bodies.

This is particularly significant for institutional adoption. BlackRock's February 2026 ETF filing flagged quantum computing as a risk to Bitcoin's integrity. Coinbase's move to offer quantum-resistant custody directly addresses that concern, potentially removing a barrier for large-scale institutional investment.

Why This Matters

The CEO of the largest US cryptocurrency exchange personally taking charge of quantum defense represents a new phase in Bitcoin's quantum story. Previous efforts were led primarily by researchers and independent developers. Armstrong's involvement signals that industry leadership is now treating quantum resistance as a competitive and existential priority.

The Consensus Challenge: Any Bitcoin protocol change must achieve rough consensus among a decentralized set of stakeholders, and past upgrades (SegWit, Taproot) have shown how contentious such processes can be. Having major industry players aligned on BIP-360 increases the chances of achieving consensus, but the timeline remains uncertain. Bitcoin has no CEO — consensus must be earned.

Armstrong's direct involvement also reflects a financial reality: the perception that Bitcoin is vulnerable to quantum attacks could trigger a loss of confidence among large investors, particularly if nation-state quantum capabilities advance faster than expected. Proactive defense is as much about market confidence as it is about cryptographic security.

Industry Context

The coalition announcement follows a rapid escalation of quantum-related activity in early 2026:

  • Google Quantum AI showed Bitcoin could fall to fewer than 500,000 physical qubits
  • Caltech/Oratomic reduced fault-tolerant QC requirements to 10,000–20,000 qubits
  • Cloudflare, Google, and IBM all converge on 2029 as a critical post-quantum deadline
  • BTQ Technologies deployed working BIP-360 transactions on testnet
  • Nobel physicist John Martinis called Bitcoin "low-hanging fruit" for quantum computers
  • StarkWare and Lightning Labs published quantum-safe solutions that work today

Armstrong's coalition represents the natural next step: organized industry coordination to turn research and prototypes into a production-grade upgrade path.
