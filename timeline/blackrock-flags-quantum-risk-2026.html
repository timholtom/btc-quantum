BlackRock Flags Quantum Risk in ETF Filing

February 2026 — Wall Street's largest asset manager acknowledges quantum computing as a potential threat to Bitcoin's network integrity.

← Back to Timeline

What Happened

BlackRock, the world's largest asset manager with over $10 trillion in assets under management, updated the registration statement for its iShares Bitcoin ETF (IBIT) to formally acknowledge quantum computing as a potential risk factor. This marked the first time a major institutional Bitcoin product explicitly flagged the quantum threat in regulatory filings.

The update came as part of BlackRock's routine regulatory compliance, but the timing coincided with growing industry awareness of quantum developments. The filing specifically mentioned that advances in quantum computing technology could "compromise the cryptographic algorithms underlying the Bitcoin network," potentially affecting the ETF's underlying asset value.

Capriole Investments' Charles Edwards argued that quantum risk should be "priced into Bitcoin" until the network implements quantum-resistant upgrades, suggesting that institutional investors were beginning to factor long-term technical risks into their Bitcoin valuations for the first time.

Why This Matters

BlackRock's acknowledgment represents a watershed moment for quantum risk awareness in traditional finance. As the sponsor of the world's largest Bitcoin ETF, BlackRock's risk disclosures carry significant weight with institutional investors and regulators. The filing signals that quantum threats are transitioning from academic curiosity to legitimate institutional concern.

This development puts pressure on other Bitcoin ETF sponsors and investment products to similarly acknowledge quantum risks in their filings. More importantly, it creates a paper trail showing that major financial institutions are aware of Bitcoin's technical vulnerabilities, which could influence insurance coverage, liability assessments, and future regulatory requirements.

The timing also reflects broader institutional nervousness following Microsoft's Majorana 1 chip announcement and Google's Quantum Echoes breakthrough in 2025. While these developments don't pose immediate threats to Bitcoin, they demonstrate accelerating progress in quantum computing capabilities.

What Happened Next

Following BlackRock's disclosure, several other major Bitcoin investment products began updating their risk statements throughout 2026. The SEC started requesting quantum risk assessments from cryptocurrency-related financial products during routine examinations. This regulatory attention accelerated discussions within the Bitcoin community about implementing BIP-360 and other quantum-resistant measures.

The disclosure also sparked increased interest in quantum-resistant Bitcoin wallet solutions, with several institutional custodians beginning to offer hybrid classical/post-quantum signature options for large Bitcoin holdings. While not consensus-level changes, these wallet-layer solutions provided institutional investors with additional quantum insurance.
