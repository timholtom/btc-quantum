Cointelegraph BIP-360 Deep Dive: What P2MR Actually Changes

March 10, 2026 — Cointelegraph publishes a comprehensive technical analysis of BIP-360, sparking a wave of mainstream coverage. Multiple outlets break down how Pay-to-Merkle-Root reduces Bitcoin's quantum exposure without delivering full post-quantum security.

← Back to Timeline

What Happened

On March 10, 2026, Cointelegraph published a major feature article titled "Bitcoin's Quantum Defense Plan: What BIP-360 Actually Changes," providing the most detailed mainstream technical breakdown of BIP-360 to date. The article was quickly picked up and analyzed by CryptoResearch Report, Bitget, SunCrypto Academy, and WorldToday, creating the largest wave of BIP-360 coverage since the proposal was first introduced.

The coverage marked a shift in narrative — from vague "quantum threat" fear-mongering to substantive technical discussion of what Bitcoin's developers are actually building and what limitations remain.

Key Technical Details from the Coverage

  • P2MR removes key path spending entirely: Unlike Taproot which offers both a key path (exposing a tweaked public key) and a script path, P2MR commits solely to the Merkle root of a script tree. No public key-based spending route exists at all.
  • All spends go through script paths: To spend a P2MR output, users reveal a script leaf and provide a Merkle proof showing it belongs to the committed root.
  • Hash-based commitments replace ECC exposure: This shifts security from elliptic curve assumptions to hash-based methods, which are far more resilient to quantum attacks.
  • Smart contracts unaffected: P2MR fully supports multisig, timelocks, conditional payments, inheritance schemes, and advanced custody — all via Tapscript Merkle trees.
  • Not full post-quantum security: BIP-360 is explicitly described as an incremental step. It reduces the attack surface significantly but does not make Bitcoin fully quantum-proof.

Three Vulnerability Classes Identified

The Cointelegraph article clearly categorized Bitcoin's quantum vulnerabilities in 2026:

  1. Reused addresses: Spending reveals the public key onchain, leaving it exposed to a future CRQC.
  2. Legacy P2PK outputs: Early Bitcoin transactions directly embedded public keys in transaction outputs (includes Satoshi's coins).
  3. Taproot key path spends: The key path exposes a tweaked public key on spend — the main theoretical weak point BIP-360 addresses.

Concurrent Coverage: St. Petersburg University

On the same day, St. Petersburg State University published an episode of "The Boson of Meaning" featuring Professor Alexey Kavokin (Head of the Spin Optics Laboratory), who discussed quantum computing timelines and crypto vulnerability. Professor Kavokin noted that up to 25% of crypto wallets could become vulnerable and that modern encryption could become obsolete in minutes once sufficiently powerful quantum computers arrive — while emphasizing that quantum security systems are also evolving in parallel.

Why It Matters

This wave of coverage is significant for several reasons:

  • First mainstream technical deep-dive: Previous coverage focused on fear or dismissal. This is the first major wave of substantive BIP-360 technical analysis in mainstream crypto media.
  • Formal roadmap acknowledgment: Cointelegraph explicitly states BIP-360 "formally puts quantum resistance on Bitcoin's road map for the first time."
  • Nuanced framing: Coverage emphasizes both what BIP-360 achieves and what it doesn't — a marked improvement over typical binary "safe/doomed" narratives.
  • Broad reach: Syndication across Cointelegraph, CryptoResearch Report, Bitget, SunCrypto Academy, and WorldToday means millions of readers exposed to the technical details.
