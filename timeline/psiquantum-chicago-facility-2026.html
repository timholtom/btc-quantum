PsiQuantum 1M-Qubit Facility Under Construction

March 2026 — Steel rises at the world's first utility-scale quantum computing campus in Chicago, targeting 1 million qubits.

← Back to Timeline

What Happened

In early March 2026, PsiQuantum's quantum computing facility at the former US Steel South Works site on Chicago's South Side entered active construction, with structural steel rising on the main facility building. The project, first announced in July 2024 and formally broken ground in late 2025, is now visibly taking shape as the world's first utility-scale quantum computing campus.

PsiQuantum's facility is designed to house a photonic quantum computer targeting 1 million qubits — orders of magnitude beyond any quantum system currently in operation. The company uses photonic (light-based) qubits, which operate at room temperature for the optical components and leverage existing semiconductor manufacturing processes for chip fabrication. Jacobs Engineering is supporting the facility's delivery.

The news immediately reignited debate in the crypto community. Multiple publications framed it as "construction begins at quantum facility big enough to break Bitcoin," while experts noted that raw qubit count alone doesn't determine cryptographic relevance — error rates, logical qubit formation, and coherence times are equally critical.

Technical Context

PsiQuantum's photonic approach differs fundamentally from the superconducting qubit designs used by Google and IBM. Photonic qubits use individual photons (particles of light) as quantum information carriers, with quantum gates implemented through optical components like beam splitters and phase shifters.

Key advantages of the photonic approach include:

  • Manufacturing scalability: Photonic chips can be fabricated in existing semiconductor foundries (GlobalFoundries manufactures PsiQuantum's chips)
  • Room-temperature optics: While some components still require cryogenic cooling, the optical processing happens at room temperature
  • Networking potential: Photons naturally travel through fiber optics, enabling modular architectures

However, photonic quantum computing faces its own challenges. Photon loss is a major issue — photons can be absorbed or scattered during computation. The probabilistic nature of photonic gates means many operations must be attempted multiple times. PsiQuantum has not yet publicly demonstrated a working prototype at meaningful scale, and the 1-million-qubit target refers to physical qubits, not the error-corrected logical qubits needed for useful computation.

The 1 Million Qubit Question

The headline number — 1 million qubits — requires careful context for Bitcoin's security:

1 million physical qubits ≠ cryptographic threat. Breaking Bitcoin's secp256k1 requires approximately 4 million logical qubits, each composed of ~1,000+ physical qubits for error correction. Even at 1 million physical qubits, PsiQuantum's system would yield only ~1,000 logical qubits at best — far short of what's needed.

That said, the significance lies in the trajectory. If PsiQuantum can demonstrate that photonic quantum computing scales to 1 million physical qubits, the path to 10 million or 100 million becomes an engineering challenge rather than a physics question. The facility is designed with expansion in mind.

Investor Connections

PsiQuantum's investor base adds a notable dimension to the Bitcoin security discussion. The company has raised over $700 million from investors including BlackRock — the same firm that manages the world's largest Bitcoin ETF (IBIT) and recently flagged quantum computing as a risk in its ETF filing. This has led some commentators to note the irony of BlackRock simultaneously investing in both Bitcoin products and a company building technology that could theoretically threaten Bitcoin.

Impact on Bitcoin's Timeline

The PsiQuantum facility represents the largest single investment in quantum computing hardware to date. Its significance for Bitcoin is primarily about acceleration of timelines:

  • Physical infrastructure: A purpose-built facility signals long-term commitment and removes infrastructure bottlenecks that have limited quantum scaling
  • Industry signal: Major capital deployment validates that industry leaders believe utility-scale quantum computing is achievable within this decade
  • Urgency argument: Provides concrete evidence for those arguing Bitcoin should accelerate post-quantum preparations
  • Reality check: Facility won't be operational for years, and demonstrated capability remains far from cryptographic relevance
Bottom line: PsiQuantum's Chicago facility is a significant infrastructure milestone that validates the quantum computing industry's ambitions. However, having a building that can house 1 million qubits is very different from having 1 million working, error-corrected qubits. The facility represents the quantum computing community putting serious money where its mouth is — but the actual threat to Bitcoin remains years to decades away.
