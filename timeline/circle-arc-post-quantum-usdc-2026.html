Circle Publishes Post-Quantum Security Roadmap for USDC & Arc

May 2026 — The USDC issuer becomes the first major stablecoin company to build quantum resistance into a blockchain from day one, publishing a phased PQC roadmap for its Arc Layer-1.

← Back to Timeline

What Happened

On May 29, 2026, Circle — the company behind USDC, the world's second-largest stablecoin — published a whitepaper detailing a phased post-quantum security roadmap for Arc, its forthcoming Layer-1 blockchain. The plan covers wallets, validators, and off-chain infrastructure, with post-quantum signature support available from Arc's mainnet launch.

USDC serves as the native gas token for Arc. The public testnet launched in October 2025, and the mainnet is targeted for later in 2026.

Technical Details

Arc will incorporate NIST-standardized lattice-based algorithms from genesis:

  • ML-DSA (FIPS 204): The primary post-quantum digital signature algorithm, formerly known as CRYSTALS-Dilithium
  • CRYSTALS-Dilithium: Lattice-based signatures designed to resist both classical and quantum attacks
  • Falcon: A compact lattice-based signature scheme, also NIST-approved, offering smaller signatures at the cost of more complex key generation

These are the same algorithms finalized by NIST in August 2024 (FIPS 203–205) and now being adopted across US government infrastructure under CNSA 2.0 mandates.

Addressing "Harvest Now, Decrypt Later"

The whitepaper explicitly addresses the HNDL threat — where adversaries record encrypted data today and wait for quantum computers to decrypt it later. By launching with post-quantum cryptography from the first block, Arc eliminates the retroactive vulnerability window that will plague every existing blockchain that must retrofit quantum resistance through hard forks.

Circle's research on quantum preparedness dates back to January 2026, suggesting months of internal work before the public roadmap.

Why This Matters for Bitcoin

While Arc is a separate blockchain from Bitcoin, Circle's move is significant for the broader crypto quantum landscape:

  • First major stablecoin PQC commitment: USDC has ~$60B+ in circulation. A major issuer building quantum-first infrastructure signals institutional seriousness about the threat timeline
  • Regulatory alignment: Arc's use of NIST-standardized algorithms positions it for compliance with evolving US cybersecurity regulations — a competitive advantage over chains that haven't started migration
  • Pressure on Bitcoin: Every new chain that launches quantum-safe from genesis makes Bitcoin's lack of post-quantum cryptography more conspicuous
  • Practical tradeoffs still unclear: Post-quantum signatures are significantly larger than classical ones. Circle hasn't detailed how Arc manages the block size, throughput, and storage costs at scale
The competitive dynamic: Circle joins Naoris Protocol (first NIST-approved quantum-resistant blockchain mainnet, April 2026) and NEAR Protocol (PQC account integration) in a growing wave of new chains building quantum resistance from the ground up. Meanwhile, Bitcoin — the largest and most valuable chain — still relies entirely on classical ECDSA and faces a multi-year consensus process to upgrade. The gap between "born quantum-safe" chains and legacy chains is widening.

  • BlackRock flags quantum risk in Bitcoin ETF filing (Feb 2026)

Circle's whitepaper adds another data point to a clear 2026 trend: institutions are no longer debating whether quantum is a threat — they're competing to be quantum-ready first.
