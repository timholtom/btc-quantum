Post-Quantum Migration & Legacy Signature Sunset BIP

July 2025 — Jameson Lopp and Christian Papathanasiou propose the most aggressive quantum defense plan yet: phase out legacy signatures and eventually freeze vulnerable coins.

← Back to Timeline

What Happened

In July 2025, a group of cryptography and blockchain experts — including well-known Bitcoin developer Jameson Lopp and BitcoinQS founder Christian Papathanasiou — published a new Bitcoin Improvement Proposal titled "Post Quantum Migration and Legacy Signature Sunset."

Unlike BIP-360 (which introduces a new quantum-resistant address type), this proposal takes a far more aggressive approach: it proposes a phased plan to completely retire Bitcoin's legacy signature schemes (ECDSA and Schnorr) that are vulnerable to quantum attacks.

The Phased Plan

Phase 1: Stop New Deposits to Vulnerable Addresses

Block transactions that send Bitcoin to older, less secure address types where public keys are already exposed on-chain. This prevents new funds from entering quantum-vulnerable addresses.

Phase 2: Freeze Vulnerable UTXOs (~5 years later)

Approximately five years after Phase 1, make it impossible to spend any Bitcoin stored in old vulnerable addresses — effectively freezing those coins. This is the most controversial aspect: it would lock up approximately 25% of all Bitcoin's unspent outputs that have exposed public keys.

Phase 3 (Under Research): Recovery Mechanism

A potential future step that would allow owners to recover frozen Bitcoin by proving ownership through cryptographic methods linked to their wallet's seed phrase. This depends on further research and community demand.

Why This Matters

This proposal is significant for several reasons:

  • First proposal to freeze coins: While BIP-360 adds quantum-resistant addresses as an option, this BIP would eventually force migration by making legacy addresses unspendable
  • Authored by prominent developers: Jameson Lopp is one of Bitcoin's most recognized infrastructure developers, lending credibility to the urgency argument
  • Quantifies the exposure: The proposal highlights that ~25% of all Bitcoin UTXOs have exposed public keys, making them immediately vulnerable once quantum computers reach sufficient capability
  • Creates a concrete timeline: Rather than open-ended preparation, this sets specific deadlines for quantum migration
The Core Argument: "The longer we postpone migration, the harder it becomes to coordinate. A clear, time-boxed plan will create certainty, align stakeholders, and minimize the risk of catastrophic quantum theft." — from the proposal

Controversy

The proposal is deeply controversial within the Bitcoin community. Freezing coins raises fundamental questions about Bitcoin's core properties:

  • Property rights: Can Bitcoin's protocol forcibly freeze someone's funds, even for security reasons?
  • Lost coins: An estimated 3-4 million BTC are in lost wallets with exposed keys. Freezing them changes Bitcoin's effective supply dynamics
  • Satoshi's coins: ~1.1 million BTC believed to belong to Satoshi Nakamoto use vulnerable P2PK addresses. This proposal would freeze them
  • Timing debate: Critics argue quantum threats are still decades away, making this premature and disruptive

As Willy Woo noted in February 2026: "I'd say it's 75% chance that lost coins will not be frozen by a protocol hard fork" — suggesting the community is far from consensus on this approach.

Relationship to BIP-360

This proposal complements BIP-360 rather than replacing it. BIP-360 (P2MR) provides the quantum-resistant address format that users would migrate to, while this "Legacy Signature Sunset" BIP provides the enforcement mechanism and timeline for that migration. Together, they represent a carrot-and-stick approach to quantum preparedness.
