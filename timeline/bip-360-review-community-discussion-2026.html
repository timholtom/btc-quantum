BIP-360 Review & Community Discussion

2026 — The Bitcoin community actively debates its first concrete quantum resistance proposal, weighing urgency against caution.

← Back to Timeline

What Happened

Throughout 2026, BIP-360 (Pay-to-Merkle-Root) entered intensive community review and discussion across multiple Bitcoin development forums. The proposal, originally drafted by Hunter Beast, Ethan Heilman, and Isabel Foxen Duke in December 2024, reached Draft v0.11.0 status and became the focal point for Bitcoin's quantum preparedness discussions.

The bitcoin-dev mailing list saw dozens of technical reviews examining P2MR's cryptographic assumptions, implementation details, and potential attack vectors. Parallel discussions on bitcointalk.org and Bitcoin Twitter (#bitcoin) brought developer concerns to the broader community, creating the first major public debate about Bitcoin's quantum future.

Key stakeholders including major Bitcoin companies, mining pools, and wallet developers began weighing in with their perspectives. The discussion revealed a community grappling with balancing proactive quantum preparation against Bitcoin's famous conservatism and resistance to premature changes.

Major Points of Debate

The community discussions centered around several critical technical and philosophical questions. The signature size tradeoffs became a major sticking point — while BIP-360's P2MR addresses protect against long-exposure quantum attacks, they don't solve the larger post-quantum signature challenge. Critics argued that implementing P2MR without addressing the 8-50KB signature sizes of post-quantum algorithms could lead to a false sense of security.

Taproot vulnerability emerged as another contentious issue. BIP-360 authors highlighted that Taproot (P2TR) outputs always expose public keys on-chain, making them vulnerable to quantum attacks even when unspent. This revelation sparked heated debates about whether Taproot adoption should be discouraged until quantum resistance is implemented, or whether the risk remains theoretical enough to ignore.

The urgency vs. caution debate reflected Bitcoin's core philosophical tensions. Quantum optimists argued that with Microsoft's Majorana topological qubits and accelerating government timelines, Bitcoin needed to act within the current decade. Quantum skeptics maintained that implementing major protocol changes for a threat that might be 20-30 years away violated Bitcoin's conservative engineering principles.

Community Response and Next Steps

The discussions ultimately strengthened rather than weakened support for quantum preparedness, but clarified the need for a more comprehensive approach. Many developers concluded that BIP-360 should be viewed as step one of a multi-phase quantum resistance strategy, not a complete solution.

Wallet developers began implementing experimental support for bc1z addresses, allowing early adopters to test P2MR functionality on testnet. Several major Bitcoin companies announced plans to integrate quantum-resistant options into their products, creating market-driven pressure for standardization.

The community consensus began coalescing around a measured approach: implement BIP-360's P2MR addresses as an optional quantum-resistant output type while simultaneously researching post-quantum signature schemes for a future BIP. This two-pronged strategy would protect against long-exposure attacks immediately while buying time to solve the more complex short-exposure attack problem.
